# Scope of work and service levels

Client-facing. Attach to the agreement. Written so a client can tell, before
signing, exactly what they are buying and exactly where the edges are.

## What is owned

**Executive support** — calendar management, inbox triage, scheduling, travel
booking and itineraries, follow-through on everything promised in either
direction.

**Project coordination** — timelines with a named critical path, next actions
with owners and dates, vendor and staff follow-up on a fixed chase schedule,
written decision records.

**Finance administration** — receipt and statement capture, filing to a parsable
naming convention, expense categorisation, monthly reconciliation, and a
year-end packet prepared for the CPA.

**Research and decision support** — option comparison, claim verification
against ranked sources, and a recommendation with a stated confidence level.

**Systems** — SOPs for recurring work, and automation of what should not be
recurring work at all.

## What is not owned

These are outside scope in every tier, and are refused in code rather than by
policy:

- **Signing anything.** Documents are prepared for the principal to execute.
- **Deleting records.** Archiving yes; destruction never.
- **Tax, legal, or investment advice.** Records are organised and categorised
  with suggested Schedule C references, always labelled as proposals for the
  CPA's review. No deductibility determination is made.
- **Acting outside the authority limits** set in the client profile.
- **Bookkeeping to close.** Records are prepared to a reconciled, tax-ready
  state and handed to the client's bookkeeper or CPA.

## Service levels

Measured on acknowledgement first, because in practice silence costs more
relationships than slowness does.

| Priority | Acknowledged within | Resolved or escalated within | Chase cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | 1 hour | 8 hours | every 8h, escalate after 1 |
| High | 4 hours | 2 business days | daily, escalate after 2 |
| Normal | 1 business day | 5 business days | every 3 days, escalate after 2 |
| Low | 3 business days | 2 weeks | weekly, escalate after 1 |

Clocks run during the client's stated working days and hours. They are enforced
by the engine, not by memory: every commitment carries its own clock, and the
daily brief surfaces anything approaching or past its limit.

Clients on the tighter end of urgency can override these per priority in their
profile rather than relying on a verbal understanding.

## Standing deliverables

| Deliverable | Cadence | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Daily brief | Each working morning | Decisions needed, the day's shape and its conflicts, overdue items, what is being chased, money position, what closed |
| Follow-up run | Each working midday | Chases sent, stalls escalated |
| Weekly summary | End of week | Shipped, in flight, blocked, needs you, plus one improvement |
| Reconciliation | Weekly | Coverage percentage and every charge lacking a document |
| CPA packet | Annually, or on request | Category totals, reconciliation position, and its own list of open questions |

## Escalation

Most things wait for the daily brief. These reach the principal immediately:

- Money leaving today that cannot be verified.
- A commitment about to be missed publicly.
- A counterparty escalating their tone — legal threat, ultimatum, formal notice.
- Anything involving the IRS, counsel, an investor, or a regulator.
- Any request that would require acting outside the authority model.
- An error on our side that has already reached someone outside.

## Authority

Set per client at onboarding, enforced before any outbound action:

- **Spend limit** — the amount that can be committed without asking.
- **Auto-send domains** — who can be corresponded with directly.
- **Review-first list** — who must always be drafted for approval first,
  regardless of the allowlist.
- **Sensitive topics** — subjects that always route to the principal even to an
  allowlisted recipient.

Anything not covered by a standing rule is drafted for approval rather than
assumed. The default posture is restrictive; authority widens only by an
explicit decision the client can point to later.

## Reporting on the service itself

Monthly: commitments logged and closed, on-time percentage against the SLA
table, reconciliation coverage, average time to acknowledge, and the count of
items escalated versus handled without interruption.

That last ratio is the honest measure of whether this is working. It should
fall over the first quarter as the authority model widens.
